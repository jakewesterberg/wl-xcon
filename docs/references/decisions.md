# Decisions and the papers they rest on

One entry per science-facing decision: what was decided, by whom and when (**Decided**), where it is
recorded (**Where**), and what it rests on (**Basis**). A published basis names its papers by key from
[library.bib](library.bib), each with the claim it supports and where in the paper that claim is. A
decision that rests on something else — an institutional rule, the PI's decision, an engineering
default — says so in **Basis** instead, and is never given a paper it does not rest on. Where a
decision combines the two, the entry says which part is whose. A **Caveat**, where there is one, is
what the literature says against or beside a decision that was made knowing it. Each heading starts
with the decision's id in the inventory of 2026-10-08 (COL for color, DISP for display, and so on).
How to add a paper or an entry: [README.md](README.md). `tests/test_references.py` checks that every
entry has its three lines, that every link names a file that exists, and that every key resolves.

## Color and luminance

### COL-01 — The default calibration is standard sRGB; how closely the panel follows it is unverified and on the warnings list
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, the PI ("Standard sRGB"; the ruling that asked for a default calibration and a warnings list is in the demo-mode spec's §14).
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 1](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5), [demo-mode spec §14](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-demo-mode-design.md#14-parked-2026-10-07-what-the-reviews-found-and-what-comes-first), [display-state note §1.6](../research/2026-10-07-engine-display-state.md#16-color-gamma-lut-s4-9-wl_xconphotometrypy-s1a-line-276-pitfalls-p19); the brainstorm notes, [§4, Batch 1](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#4-color-and-luminance).
- **Basis:** PI decision, 2026-10-07. What "standard sRGB" means: [@stokes1996standard] — primaries R (0.64, 0.33), G (0.30, 0.60), B (0.15, 0.06), D65 white (0.3127, 0.3290), the piecewise transfer (a linear segment of slope 12.92 below 0.0031308, otherwise 1.055·x^(1/2.4) − 0.055) and a reference display luminance of 80 cd/m² (Part 2, "Definition of the sRGB color space": Tables 0.1 and 0.2, Eqs. 1.2a-b). The page marks itself obsolete and superseded by [@iec1999multimedia], the standard that defines sRGB (its description page read; its text not opened). Why the panel's conformance is a warning and not an assumption: [@abuhaila2025recent] — three consumer displays calibrated to γ = 2.2 fitted γ 2.04-2.30 afterwards (Results, "Linearity and gamma function", Table 3), and performance "cannot be assumed or guaranteed" across units of one model (Discussion, "Caveats and limitations").

### COL-03 — Isoluminance needs a measured calibration in every session; equating against the calibration's named observer is enough
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, the PI (Batch 1; Batch R4, "A named observer is enough", offered against a per-animal measured null, which was recommended). The observer was named on 2026-10-08 (COL-17).
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 3](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5); the brainstorm notes, [§4, Batch 1](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#4-color-and-luminance) and [Batch R4](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#the-design-reviews-questions-asked-2026-10-07).
- **Basis:** PI decision, 2026-10-07. The named observer is a published standard: [@cie2015fundamental] — cone-fundamental-based luminous efficiency functions for 2° and 10° fields, defined as linear combinations of the L and M fundamentals (abstract), following [@sharpe2005luminous] (abstract). Partial support for photometric isoluminance in macaque cortex: [@gegenfurtner1994chromatic] — the null planes of MT neurons "varied from neuron to neuron, but all lay close to the plane of constant photometric luminance" (abstract). The literature on macaque and individual luminosity argues the other way (the caveat).
- **Caveat:** Isoluminance here is the CIE 10° standard observer's (V_F,10), not the animal's. Macaque red-green equiluminance points differ from humans' and between animals, consistent with L:M ratios near 1:1 against 2:1 [@dobkins2000comparison]; monkeys are more sensitive than humans to 15 Hz red-green isoluminant modulation in a way optics do not explain [@lindbloombrown2014spectral]; the macaque luminance plane differs from the human definition [@horwitz2015what]; "a stimulus that is equiluminant for a human may possess luminance contrast for a monkey" [@conway2014color]; even among 22 young humans, settings against this observer spanned M:L cone-contrast ratios of −6.1 to −0.7 [@he2021modeling]; per-observer flicker photometry is the published remedy where silencing luminance matters [@brainard2002display, p. 183]. Against them, MT null planes lay close to photometric isoluminance [@gegenfurtner1994chromatic]. A methods section states that the isoluminance is the standard observer's; a result that rests on it (laminar M/P/K mapping, chromatic motion) says so.

### COL-04 — Color is written device-independently; a calibration names whose luminous efficiency it used, since "a macaque V(lambda) is not a human one"; gamut clipping silently breaks isoluminance
- **Decided:** 2026-08-31, engineering, after review (S1a §12; pitfall P19, 2026-09-01); partly superseded by COL-01 to COL-03, which load color on the default calibration outside recording and accept a named human observer (inventory disagreements 6 and 7).
- **Where:** [S1a §12](../superpowers/specs/2026-08-31-S1a-vocabulary-design.md#12-added-2026-08-31-after-review--what-the-graph-could-not-see), "Colour, device-independently"; [pitfalls P19](../pitfalls.md#expanded-notes) (the table and the expanded note).
- **Basis:** Engineering default. Supporting facts: xyY is CIE chromaticity plus luminance [@cie2018colorimetry] (abstract: chromaticity coordinates, standard observers); DKL is a space of modulations about a white point, with an isoluminant plane [@derrington1984chromatic] (abstract), and its coordinates depend on the background [@brainard1996cone] (p. 564; p. 575). "A macaque V(λ) is not a human one": [@dobkins2000comparison] — macaque red-green equiluminance points showed relatively greater sensitivity to green than humans', consistent with L:M cone ratios near 1:1 in macaques and 2:1 in humans (abstract); [@horwitz2015what] — macaque detection thresholds for flicker lay on a plane that differed from the formal definition of luminance, indicating "a different spectral sensitivity than that of the average human observer" (the text on the human-macaque difference, around Fig. 3); [@lindbloombrown2014spectral] (abstract). Clipping: [@brainard1996cone] — "There is no guarantee that these differential coordinates can be achieved within the gamut of a physically realizable device" (p. 572).

### COL-06 — Color spaces: xyY (absolute); cone contrast about the background, for cone-isolating stimuli; DKL defined in cone-contrast terms with one stated normalization
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, engineering, from the science review ("Taken without asking, from the reviews"), in the spec the PI approved that day.
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 4](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5); the brainstorm notes, [§4](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#4-color-and-luminance) ("Settled before the element") and ["Taken without asking, from the reviews"](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#the-design-reviews-questions-asked-2026-10-07).
- **Basis:** Engineering default resting on published definitions. [@cie2018colorimetry] — the CIE's tristimulus values and chromaticity coordinates (abstract). [@brainard1996cone] — cone contrast is each cone's change in excitation divided by the background's, Eq. A.4.1 (p. 564); DKL is built on the same idea (p. 563), and its normalization constants "must be explicitly specified whenever the space is used" (p. 575). [@derrington1984chromatic] — the luminance, constant-S and constant-L&M axes through a white point (abstract), drawing on [@macleod1979chromaticity] (abstract: a constant-luminance chromaticity diagram of cone excitations).

### COL-07 — DKL: the luminance axis in luminance contrast, the color axes in pooled cone contrast
- **Decided:** 2026-10-08, the PI (A2 Q1: "Hybrid", offered against pooled cone contrast, Psychtoolbox `DKLDemo.m`'s per-axis scaling, and no DKL); open at the inventory's commit.
- **Where:** [A2 research note §2](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#2-dkl-normalizations-in-use) and its [Q1](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#questions-for-the-pi-the-agents-framing-to-be-asked-in-plain-terms); [engine spec §7.4](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5), which requires one stated normalization; the brainstorm notes, "The morning after (asked 2026-10-08)", on engine-b.
- **Basis:** [@derrington1984chromatic] — the space: a luminance axis and two isoluminant axes, "constant B" and "constant R & G", through a white point (abstract). [@brainard1996cone] — luminance contrast is the natural scaling for the luminance axis, and there is none for the two color axes (p. 571); scaling each axis to unit response for its isolating stimulus of unit pooled cone contrast does not depend on apparatus or observer (p. 572; pooled cone contrast is Eq. A.4.2, p. 568); the constants and the background must be stated whenever the space is used (p. 575); Derrington et al.'s own normalization, unit response at the monitor's largest in-gamut modulation, "makes the definition of the color space apparatus dependent" (pp. 571-572), and angular coordinates depend on the normalization (p. 574). [@conway2014color] — the true geometry of cone-opponent spaces "is contingent on the instrument used to generate them" ("Questions of Perception"). Taking Brainard's natural choice for one axis and his adopted one for the other two is the PI's decision among the options offered; no single paper prescribes the combination, so a methods section states it. Its consequence, that the axes then carry different units and the gamut check needs a limit per axis, is the research note's (§2).

### COL-08 — Realizability of every color is tested by full conversion to primary weights in [0, 1], replacing the `magnitude() <= max_cone_contrast` gate
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, engineering, from the science review (finding 7b), in the revised spec.
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 4](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5); [A2 research note §0](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#0-corrections-to-current-repo-text) item 3 and [§3](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#3-cone-contrast-to-primary-weights).
- **Basis:** Engineering default on a published method. [@brainard2002display] — the primary intensities that produce desired cone coordinates are w = M⁻¹s, with M the cone excitations of the measured primaries (pp. 177-178, Eq. 14), and intensities are numbers in [0, 1] (p. 173, Eq. 1). [@brainard1996cone] — a requested cone-contrast or DKL modulation carries "no guarantee" of lying within a device's gamut (p. 572).

### COL-09 — The default background is black; a task using DKL, cone contrast or Weber contrast declares its own non-black background; xyY colors and Michelson contrast run on black
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, the PI (Batch 2, "Black"; Batch 3, "It must declare one").
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 5](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5) and [§4.12](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#412-contrast); the brainstorm notes, [§4, Batches 2 and 3](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#4-color-and-luminance).
- **Basis:** PI decision for black, 2026-10-07. Why background-relative quantities need a non-black background: [@brainard1996cone] — cone contrast "makes sense primarily when there is a background with respect to which contrast may be computed" (pp. 563-564), and DKL coordinates require "an explicit specification of the cone excitation coordinates of the background" (p. 575); [@pelli2013measuring] — Weber contrast is the target's difference from the background luminance L_b divided by L_b, while Michelson contrast uses only the maximum and minimum luminance (Introduction, Eqs. 1-2). On black, L_b = 0 and the background's cone excitations are zero, so Weber and cone contrast are undefined (arithmetic, not a citation).

### COL-10 — The calibration is measured at a reference background; limits for a task's own background come from the full conversion, not a stored maximum
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, engineering default, in the engine spec.
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 5](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5).
- **Basis:** Engineering default. Supporting: [@brainard1996cone] — cone-contrast coordinates depend on the background (p. 564), and "the construction of the conversion matrix depends on the cone excitation coordinates of the background" (p. 571); [@brainard2002display] — the conversion to primary intensities is a function of the target's cone coordinates (p. 178, Eq. 14), so a contrast limit stored for one background does not carry over to another.

### COL-11 — The transfer is a measured table per channel; one calibration for the whole panel, the stereoscope's two eyes assumed equal; an imbalance appears as eye dominance, so dichoptic tasks counterbalance the masked eye
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, the PI (Batch 1, "A measured table"; Batch 3, "One for the panel"; Batch R4, "Assume equal, as decided", offered against measuring each eye's mirror path, which was recommended).
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 6](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5); the brainstorm notes, [§4, Batches 1 and 3](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#4-color-and-luminance) and [Batch R4](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#the-design-reviews-questions-asked-2026-10-07).
- **Basis:** PI decision, 2026-10-07. The measured per-channel table: [@brainard2002display] — each channel's output is a measured function of its digital value, inverted to find the value for a desired intensity (pp. 177-179, "Gamma correction"). The stated limitation: [@zhou2013effect] — prior studies indicate "that an interocular luminance difference can induce a functional imbalance between the two eyes in normal observers, reducing binocular function, altering interocular interactions and shifting sensory dominance to the eye with the higher luminance level" (Introduction); in their data a 1 log-unit attenuation of one eye moved the balance point from 1.03 to 0.91 (Results, "Normal subjects"). Assuming the halves equal runs against published panel measurements (the caveat).
- **Caveat:** Published consumer-OLED uniformity is 4-7% worst case, the left half worse on every consumer display tested [@abuhaila2025recent, Results "Luminance uniformity", Fig. 8], so "equal halves" is an assumption V9's per-half photometry checks. The stated eye-dominance effect is supported and small at a few percent (a 1 log-unit attenuation moved the balance point from 1.03 to 0.91 [@zhou2013effect]). Not stated in the spec: a dimmer eye's signal is also delayed (about 38 ms in V1 for a 1.5 log-unit filter [@chadnova2018interocular]), the Pulfrich effect, which counterbalancing the masked eye does not remove from a motion-in-depth task. At a few percent the delay should be far smaller; no measurement at that size was found. A motion-in-depth task checks V9's measured difference first.

### COL-13 — Overall brightness is capped so the panel's ABL never engages, at a level V9 measures (including one half's fill dimming the other); a display exceeding it is refused and the record states the cap
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, the PI (Batch 2, "Stay below it").
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 7](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5); [S0 §5.4](../superpowers/specs/2026-08-31-S0-hosts-and-hardware-design.md#54-panel-acceptance-test--written-now-before-the-panel-exists) item 2; [S4 §9](../superpowers/specs/2026-08-31-S4-stimulus-presentation-design.md#9-stimulus-calibration-and-the-id-the-manifest-already-wants); the brainstorm notes, [§4, Batch 2](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#4-color-and-luminance).
- **Basis:** PI decision, 2026-10-07, supported by [@dimigen2026advantages] — ABL "is an inherent technical limitation of OLED panels", and monitors "vary with regard to the APL at which this mechanism kicks in" (Introduction); on an ASUS PG27AQDM with its uniform-brightness mode off, luminance "changed drastically with the APL" above the 40% brightness setting (about 140 cd/m²), and "at settings of 40% or less, no ABL occurred" (Results, "Auto-brightness limiting behavior"), so operating below a measured level prevents ABL. [@abuhaila2025recent] — ABL is triggered in proportion to the average picture level (Metrics, "Filling factor"). [@brainard2002display] — the CRT forerunner, "dynamic brightness stabilization", makes each pixel depend on the whole frame and is detected with a fixed test patch and a changing surround (p. 180, failure 6 and test 3), the test V9 applies across the two halves. S0 §5.4 item 2 credited Abu Haila et al. with an earlier study's fill-factor finding (a Sony PVM-2541, quoted in their Introduction); corrected there on 2026-10-08.

### COL-14 — Output is 10-bit (1024 levels per channel), verified at V1; dithering, if it is the fallback, is deterministic (so the exact drawer models it) and on the warnings list
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, the PI (Batch 1, "10-bit, verified at V1").
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 8](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5); the brainstorm notes, [§4, Batch 1](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#4-color-and-luminance).
- **Basis:** PI decision, 2026-10-07, supported by [@brainard2002display] — "The 256-level quantization of the 8-bit DACs is too coarse to measure threshold on a uniform background"; finer steps come from more-than-8-bit DACs or from spatial or temporal dithering (p. 183, "Fine control of contrast"); [@pelli1991accurate] — accurate low contrast needs more than 8 bits, and their attenuator renders low contrasts with 12-bit accuracy (abstract); [@tyler1997colour] — bit-stealing gives 11-12 bits of luminance resolution, cutting the smallest mid-range step from 1.5% to below 0.2% (abstract). Dithering as a fallback: [@allard2008noisybit] — random dithering is perceptually equivalent to continuous luminance resolution at ordinary spatiotemporal resolutions (abstract). Their method is random; making the fallback deterministic so the exact drawer can model it is an engineering choice with no paper behind it (none of these argues for deterministic over random dithering).

### COL-15 — Cone fundamentals: one lab-wide default, Stockman & Sharpe 10° (CIE 2006); a task may name the 2° set for foveal work; no macaque set; the spectroradiometer's spectra are stored so any set converts
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, the PI (Batch R4; Batch R5, the 10° standard "unless there is a better macaque alternative"); confirmed 2026-10-08 (A2 Q4, "Human 10°, switchable later", after a deeper search; COL-16).
- **Where:** [engine spec §7 item 9](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5); [A2 research note §0](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#0-corrections-to-current-repo-text), [§1](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#1-cone-fundamentals-data) and [§5](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#5-macaque-vs-human); the brainstorm notes, [Batches R4 and R5](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#the-design-reviews-questions-asked-2026-10-07), and [§4, Batch 4](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md#4-color-and-luminance), which named the 2° set as the example, superseded.
- **Basis:** PI decision, 2026-10-07, on a literature check. [@stockman2000spectral] — L- and M-cone spectral sensitivities for 2° and 10° central fields (abstract). [@cie2006fundamental] — the CIE 2006 cone fundamentals, derived from the Stiles & Burch 10° color-matching functions following Stockman & Sharpe, tabulated for 10° and 2°, adjustable to any field size from 1° to 10° and to age (abstract). [@stockman2023formulae] — the CIE "sanctioned the cone spectral sensitivity estimates of Stockman and Sharpe" as "physiologically-relevant" standards (Abstract; Introduction); standard macular density at 460 nm is 0.350 for 2° and 0.095 for 10°, the pigment being "largely absent by about 10 deg" ("Formulae for standard observers", "Macular and lens optical density spectra"), which is why 2° suits foveal stimuli. Macaque and human cones: [@schnapf1988spectral] — red and green cones "from a human retina were virtually identical to those of macaque cones" (abstract; one human retina, L and M only); [@baylor1987spectral] — macaque peaks near 430, 531 and 561 nm, and human color matching well predicted from the monkey cones after correcting for pre-retinal absorption (abstract); [@horwitz2015what] — cone absorption spectra "are very similar" in the two species (the opening section). Common practice in macaque work: [@li2022cone] — macaque V1 cone-isolating and DKL stimuli computed with the Stockman & Sharpe 10° fundamentals (Methods, "Cone-isolating calculation", "DKL Hue"); [@conway2014color] — "human cone fundamentals are routinely used to define color stimuli for testing responses of cells in monkeys" (section "MT"); [@spitschan2018method] recommend the CIE 2006 fundamentals ("Spectral sensitivities assumed"). Storing spectra: [@brainard2002display] — a spectroradiometer is needed to use any description of color vision other than the CIE system's (p. 185, "Instrumental considerations"). "No macaque set" is an absence the searches of 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08 found ("not found", not "does not exist": the A2 research note §5), not a published claim.
- **Caveat:** For nonhuman observers "it is crucial to customize the rendering calculations for the spectral sensitivities appropriate for the species" [@brainard2002display, p. 183], and the macaque lens is about 60% of the human density [@horwitz2015what]. The unadjusted human observer is nonetheless the field's routine practice [@conway2014color; @li2022cone], and the 2026-10-08 computation put the effect near the CIE's own 2° against 10° difference and below the spread between animals. The observer's parameters are recorded and the macaque lens or macular setting can be switched on later.

### COL-16 — Cone fundamentals: the CIE 10° observer, unadjusted for macaques
- **Decided:** 2026-10-08, the PI (A2 Q4: "Human 10°, switchable later", offered against a macaque lens at 60%, lens plus no macular pigment, and no macular pigment only), after a literature search; the 10° default itself on 2026-10-07 (the brainstorm notes' N§R5).
- **Where:** [engine spec §7.9](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md#7-color-and-luminance-n4-nr4-nr5), [A2 research note §5](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#5-macaque-vs-human) and its [Q4](../research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md#questions-for-the-pi-the-agents-framing-to-be-asked-in-plain-terms); the brainstorm notes, "The morning after (asked 2026-10-08)", on engine-b.
- **Basis:** [@stockman2000spectral] — the 10° cone fundamentals themselves (Table 2, pp. 1732-1734); that the CIE 2006 observer is this set is [@cie2006fundamental]'s statement (abstract). [@schnapf1988spectral] — the red and green cones of one human retina were "virtually identical" to macaque cones (abstract). [@baylor1987spectral] — macaque cone peaks near 430, 531 and 561 nm, and human color matching well predicted from the monkey cones (abstract). [@horwitz2015what] — fitting rhesus detection data needs a macaque lens about 60% of the average human lens density, put down to the monkeys being younger and smaller (the text on Fig. 3); [@lindbloombrown2014spectral] — monkeys had lower blue-to-green threshold ratios than humans at 0.5-7°, "consistent with a lower lens optical density" (abstract). Not adjusting for it is the PI's decision. It rests on the search's own computation that a macaque lens or macular setting changes stimuli by about the CIE's own 2° vs 10° difference (summarized in the brainstorm notes; not a published result), and on that being smaller than the spread between animals. The spread is published — macular pigment's central peak 0.42-1.0 across monkey retinas, falling to low, fairly constant levels within 1 mm [@snodderly1984macular] (abstract), and zeaxanthin differing up to fourfold between individual monkeys [@handelman1991biological] (abstract) — but comparing it with the computed effect is the search's own. Common practice uses the unadjusted human observer: [@conway2014color] (section "MT"); [@li2022cone] (Methods). The observer's parameters are recorded so the setting can be switched on later, which the standards provide for: fundamentals for other conditions are derived from the low-density absorbances with lens, macular and pigment densities [@cie2006fundamental] (abstract), and [@stockman2023formulae] give formulae for non-standard observers with individual macular, lens and photopigment densities (Abstract; "Formulae for non-standard observers and for changes with eccentricity").
- **Caveat:** For nonhuman observers "it is crucial to customize the rendering calculations for the spectral sensitivities appropriate for the species" [@brainard2002display, p. 183], and the macaque lens is about 60% of the human density [@horwitz2015what]. The unadjusted human observer is nonetheless the field's routine practice [@conway2014color; @li2022cone], and the 2026-10-08 computation put the effect near the CIE's own 2° against 10° difference and below the spread between animals. The observer's parameters are recorded and the macaque lens or macular setting can be switched on later.

### COL-21 — A calibration never expires but carries its age; past 30 days it is a warning
- **Decided:** 2026-10-07, the PI (Batch 3, in his words: "never expires, but there is an age that is associated with the calibration. A warning pops after 30 days").
