**Goal:** A task can name a color in cone terms (a `DKL` color or a `ConeContrast`) and have it mean one thing: a contrast about its background in the lab's one observer, the CIE 2006 10° cone fundamentals, turned into light through the session's calibration, refused where the panel cannot make it, and drawn by the exact drawer down to the output levels the panel is driven with.

**Architecture:** A new module, `wl_xcon/cones.py`, holds the observer: the CIE's own 10° table, bundled unmodified in `wl_xcon/cie/` under CC BY-SA 4.0 and read only when its sha256 is the CIE's (ADR-0012), the CIE's LMS-to-XYZ_F,10 transformation and V_F,10, and the observer's parameters as a record writes them. `photometry` gains `ConeContrast`, a calibration's measured `Spectra`, `Calibration.cones` (CIE XYZ to cone excitations for the lights a panel makes: the CIE's matrix inverted on the default, the primaries' spectra on a measured record) and `cone_xyz`, which reads a `DKL` number as the PI's hybrid; `unrealizable` becomes full conversion against a background, with each channel's undriven light as its floor, and `max_cone_contrast` goes. `check` refuses a cone color on a background that is or can be black, or between two eyes' backgrounds that differ; reads every cone color at every value its parameters can take, against every lit value of the background; retires engine build B's `dkl-on-default` (a cone color that claims no isoluminance now loads on the default in training and piloting, under `color-on-default`, whose sentence says how it converted) and `unstated-observer`; and holds a Weber light and a grating's bars to the panel's reach. `screen.resolve` converts a cone color against the background through a calibration, `exact.output_levels` turns a drawn eye into output levels through the calibration's measured transfer, and `config.json` records how a session's cone colors became light. `visual_search` gets the gray its colors are defined against.

**Tech Stack:** Python 3.11-3.13; dataclasses; numpy (already a dependency, ADR-0004) for the cone tables and the drawer; `hashlib`, `importlib.resources` and `json` from the standard library; pytest. **No new dependency.** Two data files of the CIE's ship as package data under their own license (ADR-0012).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-design.md` (approved 2026-10-07) §4.3, §4.9, §7 (items 3-6 and 9, and build B's dated note), §21 and §24's row for A, with the PI's answers in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-engine-brainstorm-notes.md` (cited N§k): §4 batches 1-4, R4, R5 and "The morning after"; the research note `docs/research/2026-10-08-engine-a2-color-research.md`; ADR-0011. ## What the PI should know

Ten things this plan does or does not do that are not in a Question, in plain terms.

1. **The search task still does not run on the default calibration.** Your rule (2026-10-07) is that isoluminance needs a measured calibration in every kind of session, and its red and green are isoluminant. What A2 changes is that it now runs on a *measured* calibration, which needs a gray behind it (Q1). Its training variant is unaffected.
2. **Other cone-defined colors now run on the default for training and piloting**, with the warning, where build B refused them until A2: a DKL color that is not isoluminant, and any cone contrast. They convert through the CIE's matrix as you chose (Q2), and the warning a person accepts says it is "a use outside the CIE's definition", so `warnings.jsonl` records it. Recording still refuses them on the default.
3. **A cone contrast never "claims isoluminance".** Your rule names a DKL color with `lum=0`. A cone contrast that raises only the S cones is isoluminant by arithmetic under V_F,10, but it is written as cone contrasts, not as a luminance claim, so on the default it runs in training and piloting with the warning rather than being refused (call 15). If you want S-cone stimuli held to a measured calibration too, that is a one-line change.
4. **A measured calibration must carry its spectra to show any cone-defined color.** The spectroradiometer you chose (COL-20) gives them; a record without them still runs plain colors and grays, and refuses every DKL or cone-contrast color by name rather than guessing (call 8).
5. **The observer is the CIE's published 10° table, not rebuilt from its parts.** Your Q4 answer was "Human 10°, switchable later", described as "built from the CIE's parts". The CIE publishes only the finished table as data; the parts (lens, macular pigment, photopigment density) exist as formulae in Stockman and Rider (2023) [@stockman2023formulae], which reproduce the table "with little error", not exactly. A2 uses the table you chose in Q3 and records the parts' standard values with every session; building the observer from the formulae comes with the switch, when a macaque lens or macular setting is first turned on (call 1).
6. **A task cannot yet name the 2° cone set for foveal work** (your 2026-10-07 answer, spec §7.9). No task needs it, and when one does there is a question for you then: your Q5 answer put isoluminance on the 10° V_F,10 rather than "follow the task", so a 2° task would mix 2° cones with 10° luminance unless you say otherwise. Filed, not asked now (call 2).
7. **Bundling the CIE's tables reopens ADR-0004**, whose 2026-09-26 amendment says any copyleft asset does: CC BY-SA 4.0 is share-alike. ADR-0012 records your Q3 answer as that reopening. The files are never changed (a changed table would be "adapted material" and carry the license); values between the CIE's 5 nm points are computed in memory, never saved. Whether an unchanged CSV beside Apache-2.0 code is fine is the research note's reading, not legal advice (UNVERIFIED).
8. **"The same DKL number on every rig" holds only roughly.** Backgrounds are written in CIE 1931 terms (a D65 gray), and two panels with different spectra make that gray with slightly different cone excitations, so the red-green direction differs a little between rigs (the research note computed an isoluminant M:L ratio of -2.21 against -2.26 on two modeled displays). COL-32's caveat says so; a methods section states the background.
9. **The checker now refuses a contrast the panel cannot show**: a Weber light or a grating bar brighter than the panel's white (for example a full-contrast grating on a gray above half the default's 80 cd/m²). Over another stimulus it is caught when the frame is drawn, not at load (call 18).
10. **"The record carries two luminances" (your Q5 answer) is not built here.** Every luminance a task writes stays in cd/m² on CIE V(λ); V_F,10 enters only as a contrast (isoluminance, the DKL luminance axis). The second luminance per light belongs to the screen log, build F (call 21).

## Questions for the PI

Each is asked in plain terms, with a recommendation first. Q1 and Q2 are answered before Task 3 starts; nothing before Task 3 depends on them.

**Q1. What gray goes behind the search task's red and green?** Its red and green are written as DKL colors, which are contrasts about the background, and your rule (2026-10-07, "It must declare one") refuses such a color on the black default background. So from this build the search task needs a gray, or it loads nowhere. (It still does not run on the default calibration, since its colors are isoluminant; the gray matters once a panel is measured.) Your own pop-out papers used "a uniform gray background" [@westerberg2020priming] (Materials and Methods), and in 2023 the items and the gray were all 2.8 cd/m² on a CRT [@westerberg2023feedforward] (Methods, "Task design: Pop-out search").
- **A (recommended): a fixed gray, D65 white at 20 cd/m², written in the task.** The fixation point's starting 40 cd/m² stays twice as bright as the gray, and the red and green (±0.08) fit comfortably: on the sRGB standard's colors at that gray, red-green reaches at least ±0.16 (computed 2026-10-09; that reach is the same at any gray up to half the white). Changing it later is a reviewed edit to the task.
- B: a fixed gray at 40 cd/m², half the default's white. As much room for color as A, but the fixation point's starting 40 would then match it and vanish, so that start (your 2026-10-07 answer) would have to change too.
- C: a setting a person can change live, starting at 20 cd/m² and bounded 5-40. The checker then holds the red and green to the panel at both ends of the range.
- D: your 2023 design's 2.8 cd/m².
- *Builds:* Task 3 (`tasks/visual_search.py`'s `BACKGROUND`; COL-33).

**Q2. Does the search task's training version show the same gray?** The training version uses the sRGB standard's red and green at their own brightness settings (your 2026-10-08 answer) on black, and it runs on the default calibration in training and piloting.
- **A (recommended): yes, the same gray**, so the animal trains on the scene it will later be tested on. Its red and green and their settings are unchanged; nothing about where it may run changes (computed 2026-10-09: the checker gives it the same two warnings on the gray as on black).
- B: keep it on black.
- *Builds:* Task 3 (`tasks/visual_search_training.py`).

## Calls taken in this plan

Engineering decisions made without asking; each says what it costs if wrong.

1. **The observer is the CIE's 10° table, as published; its parts are recorded, not built from** (Q3, Q4). The CIE publishes no part (lens, macular pigment, photopigment absorbance) as a data set (its data-tables page, read 2026-10-09); Stockman and Rider (2023) give them as formulae, CC BY 4.0, reproducing the table "with little error". Building from the formulae now would make A2's fundamentals differ slightly from the table chosen in Q3 and leave code nothing calls. `cones.CIE2006_10` records the standard values: field 10°, peak photopigment optical densities 0.38, 0.38, 0.30, macular density 0.095 at 460 nm, lens density 1.7649 at 400 nm, all as Stockman and Rider state the CIE standard's. Filed: XC-??? (observer from its parts). Cost if wrong: one task when the switch is wanted.
2. **A task's 2° choice is not built** (spec §7.9, COL-15): no task needs it, and Q5's answer leaves its luminosity a question for then. Filed: XC-??? (2° for foveal work). Cost: one task and one question later.
3. **Two CIE files ship, both unmodified: the 10° cone table and the 10° XYZ_F functions.** The second is never read at runtime; a test holds the CIE's LMS-to-XYZ_F,10 matrix to it, since Q2 rests on that matrix and the CIE 170-2 report that prints it was not opened (the matrix's digits are Stockman and Rider's Eq. 15, CC BY 4.0, read 2026-10-09). The V_F,10 data set is not bundled: it is that file's ȳ column exactly (compared 2026-10-09). Cost: 17 KB and a second file under BY-SA.
4. **A bundled table is read only when its sha256 is the one the CIE's metadata gives**, every time it is first read in a process, and refused by a sentence otherwise. The metadata's own checksum (not the data set page's MD5, which is stale for the 10° table: page `c92c94a7…`, file `c2e606fd…`, checked 2026-10-09) is pinned in `cones.CIE_FILES` and a test holds the two to the metadata. Cost: none.
5. **Between the CIE's 5 nm points the fundamentals are linear** (its metadata's declared `interpolationMethod`), **zero outside 390-830 nm and the S cone zero beyond 615 nm** (its declared `extrapolationMethod`, and its blank cells there), computed in memory onto the wavelengths a light was measured at. Never written: a resampled table would be an adaptation (ADR-0012). Cost: none.
6. **A calibration's spectra are what the instrument sampled**: each primary's spectral radiance at full drive, W·sr⁻¹·m⁻²·nm⁻¹, at strictly rising wavelengths covering 390-780 nm, never more than 5 nm apart, finite, none negative and not all zero; integrated by the trapezoid rule over their own wavelengths. Beyond 780 nm the L cones take 2.0e-6 of an equal-energy light's excitation and the M cones 2.0e-7 (computed from the bundled table, 2026-10-09), so a spectrum may stop there. Cost: a record build J writes on a coarser grid is refused, and J resamples or the bound moves.
7. **A record's spectra are not checked against its primaries' xyY.** Both come from one measurement in build J's tool, which can compare them; doing it here needs the CIE 1931 color-matching functions as a third CIE file. Filed: XC-??? (spectra against primaries). Cost if wrong: a hand-made record whose spectra and xyY disagree converts cone colors wrongly, unnoticed.
8. **A measured calibration without spectra converts no cone color**: each one is refused as `unrealizable-color`, saying why. The alternative, the default's CIE matrix applied to measured xyY, was not asked of the PI (Q2 was about the default) and would put the default's approximation into recording sessions. Cost: a colorimeter-only record shows grays and xyY colors only.
9. **A measured calibration names its observer, or it does not load; `unstated-observer` retires.** Isoluminance is now the lab observer's V_F,10 (Q5), named in code and recorded with each session, so the finding's question (whose luminous efficiency?) has one answer; what the record's `observer` still names is whose photometry its own cd/m² are, and an empty one is refused when the calibration is built. Cost: one finding code gone (no record or task uses it).
10. **A cone color in a trial whose two eyes' backgrounds differ is refused** (`cone-color-two-backgrounds`): it would be two different lights. `screen.resolve` raises on it as the backstop. Filed: XC-??? (per-eye cone colors). Cost: a dichoptic cone-color task on unequal backgrounds waits for that item.
11. **A cone color is checked against every lit light the background can be**: each choice, each corner of a `Gray`'s or an `xyY`'s ranges; a black value is left to `cone-color-on-black`. Exact for a `Gray` background (the conversion is bilinear in the background's luminance and the color's components, so corners bound it); for a background whose chromaticity is a range, corners only. Filed: XC-??? (ranged background chromaticity). Cost: such a task could load a color the panel cannot make at an interior chromaticity, and the drawer's levels refuse it when drawn.

14. **A `DKL` color's axes, as built** (Q1, COL-07; COL-32): `lum` scales the background in every cone (luminance contrast, under V_F,10 the same number); `l_m` moves L and M at the ratio that holds V_F,10 still, S fixed, scaled to unit pooled cone contrast; `s_lm` moves S alone; `+l_m` raises L (toward red), `+s_lm` raises S (toward violet), `+lum` brightens; the three add.
```python
"""The cone fundamentals every cone color converts through (engine spec §7.9; engine build A2).

**One lab-wide observer: the CIE 2006 10° cone fundamentals**, Stockman and Sharpe's, unadjusted
for macaques (the PI, 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08: A2's Q4, "Human 10°, switchable later"; COL-15,
COL-16). Its table is the CIE's own data set, bundled unmodified in `wl_xcon/cie/` under CC BY-SA
4.0 (A2's Q3, "Bundle the CIE's files"; ADR-0012), and **read only after its sha256 matches the one
the CIE's metadata gives**, so a table that changed on disk is refused rather than believed. Its
parameters are `CIE2006_10`'s, recorded with every session that converts a cone color
(`Observer.record`), so a macaque lens or macular setting switched on later is told apart.

**Isoluminance and the DKL luminance axis are the CIE's cone-based V_F,10** (A2's Q5, "Cone-based
V(λ), 10°"; COL-17): `V_F10`, the luminance row of the CIE's transformation from these
fundamentals to XYZ_F,10 (`LMS_TO_XYZ_F10`), which `tests/test_cones.py` holds to the CIE's own
tabulated XYZ_F,10. cd/m² stays on CIE V(λ): nothing here names an absolute luminance.

Read when a calibration is converted, at load; never inside a frame.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import hashlib
from dataclasses import dataclass
from importlib import resources

import numpy as np

#: The CIE's files this package carries, each by the sha256 its metadata JSON gives
#: (`checksums`, read 2026-10-09; the data set page's MD5 for the LMS table is stale, the
#: metadata's is not). `CIE_lms_cf_10deg.csv` is DOI 10.25039/CIE.DS.nxsqeri8 (CIE 170-1:2006,
#: Table 6.2); `CIE_cfb_stv_10deg.csv`, the XYZ_F,10 functions, is DOI 10.25039/CIE.DS.dm6qiig7
#: (CIE 170-2:2015, Table 10.8), carried so a test can hold `LMS_TO_XYZ_F10` to it.
CIE_FILES = {
    "CIE_lms_cf_10deg.csv": "bd64f1f688a4b319c6d3fa6b31770a32eaaaaea0eed532befd0424f96304db18",
    "CIE_cfb_stv_10deg.csv": "9019a35f8f51215e245f818e87d4251147d1925a8fbe9a49944fe7f011f16e38",
}

#: The cone fundamentals' table.
TABLE = "CIE_lms_cf_10deg.csv"

#: The CIE's transformation from the 10° cone fundamentals (energy, each peaking at 1) to its
#: cone-fundamental-based tristimulus functions XYZ_F,10, row by row: x̄_F,10, ȳ_F,10, z̄_F,10.
#: As printed in Stockman and Rider (2023), Eq. 15 (CC BY 4.0, read 2026-10-09 in its Europe PMC
#: full text, PMC10946592); CIE 170-2:2015 itself was not opened. Applied to the bundled table it
#: reproduces the CIE's tabulated XYZ_F,10 at every 5 nm wavelength within 1.4e-6 (computed
#: 2026-10-09; `tests/test_cones.py`).
LMS_TO_XYZ_F10 = (
    (1.93986443, -1.34664359, 0.43044935),
    (0.69283932, 0.34967567, 0.0),
    (0.0, 0.0, 2.14687945),
)

#: V_F,10, the CIE's cone-based luminosity for a 10° field: this many of L and of M, and no S
#: (A2's Q5). It is `LMS_TO_XYZ_F10`'s luminance row, and the CIE tabulates the same function
#: as its own data set (DOI 10.25039/CIE.DS.8mrru44q), identical to that row's column.
V_F10 = (LMS_TO_XYZ_F10[1][0], LMS_TO_XYZ_F10[1][1])


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class Observer:
    """Whose cones a cone color is in, and the parameters that make it that observer, recorded
    with each session (A2's Q4: "its parameters recorded").

    The densities are the CIE standard's for its field size, as Stockman and Rider (2023) state
    them ("Macular and lens optical density spectra"; "Formulae for standard observers"): the peak
    photopigment optical densities of the L, M and S cones, the macular pigment's density at
    460 nm and the lens's at 400 nm."""

    name: str
    #: The DOI of the table it is.
    data: str
    field_deg: float
    peak_optical_density: tuple[float, float, float]
    macular_density_460nm: float
    lens_density_400nm: float
    #: The luminosity function behind isoluminance and the DKL luminance axis.
    luminosity: str

    def record(self) -> dict:
        """The observer as a session record writes it."""
        return {
            "name": self.name,
            "data": self.data,
            "field_deg": self.field_deg,
            "peak_optical_density": list(self.peak_optical_density),
            "macular_density_460nm": self.macular_density_460nm,
            "lens_density_400nm": self.lens_density_400nm,
            "luminosity": self.luminosity,
        }


#: **The lab's observer** (COL-15, COL-16): the CIE 2006 10° standard observer as published, its
#: lens and macular pigment the human standard's. A macaque setting, when the PI switches one on,
#: is a different `Observer` built from these parts (XC-??? (observer from its parts)).
CIE2006_10 = Observer(
    name="CIE 2006 10°",
    data="10.25039/CIE.DS.nxsqeri8",
    field_deg=10.0,
    peak_optical_density=(0.38, 0.38, 0.30),
    macular_density_460nm=0.095,
    lens_density_400nm=1.7649,
    luminosity="V_F,10",
)


def cie_file(name: str) -> bytes:
    """One of the CIE's bundled files, **refused unless its sha256 is the CIE's**: an edited, a
    truncated or a re-saved table (a spreadsheet's line endings) would convert every cone color
    wrongly, and nothing downstream could tell."""
    expected = CIE_FILES[name]
    raw = resources.files("wl_xcon").joinpath("cie", name).read_bytes()
    found = hashlib.sha256(raw).hexdigest()
    if found != expected:
        raise ValueError(
            f"wl_xcon/cie/{name} is not the CIE's file: its sha256 is {found}, and the CIE's "
            f"metadata gives {expected}; restore it from the package"
        )
    return raw


def parse(raw: bytes) -> np.ndarray:
    """A CIE table's rows as numbers, one row per wavelength. A blank the CIE leaves (the S cone
    beyond 615 nm, written NaN) is zero: its metadata's declared extrapolation."""
    rows = [line.split(",") for line in raw.decode("ascii").splitlines() if line.strip()]
    return np.nan_to_num(np.array([[float(v) for v in row] for row in rows]), nan=0.0)


_TABLE: list = []


def table() -> np.ndarray:
    """The 10° cone fundamentals, (89, 4): wavelength in nm, 390 to 830 by 5, then l̄10, m̄10 and
    s̄10, each in energy units peaking at 1. Read once, on first use."""
    if not _TABLE:
        _TABLE.append(parse(cie_file(TABLE)))
    return _TABLE[0]


def fundamentals(nm) -> np.ndarray:
    """l̄10, m̄10 and s̄10 at each wavelength in `nm`, (n, 3): **linear interpolation between the
    CIE's 5 nm values**, the method its metadata declares, and zero outside 390-830 nm, its
    declared extrapolation. Computed, never written: a resampled table would be an adaptation of
    the CIE's (ADR-0012)."""
    t = table()
    at = np.asarray(nm, dtype=float)
    return np.stack([np.interp(at, t[:, 0], t[:, k], left=0.0, right=0.0) for k in (1, 2, 3)],
                    axis=-1)


def excitations(nm, radiance) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """The L, M and S excitations of a light whose spectral radiance is `radiance` at each of
    `nm`: each fundamental times the radiance, integrated by the trapezoid rule over the
    light's own wavelengths. In the radiance's units times nm; only ratios are ever read."""
    at = np.asarray(nm, dtype=float)
    weighted = fundamentals(at) * np.asarray(radiance, dtype=float)[:, None]
    steps = np.diff(at)[:, None]
    totals = ((weighted[1:] + weighted[:-1]) / 2.0 * steps).sum(axis=0)
    return (float(totals[0]), float(totals[1]), float(totals[2]))
```

```python
#: What a calibration's spectra must cover, nm: the CIE table runs 390-830, and beyond 780 nm the
#: L cones take 2.0e-6 of an equal-energy light's excitation and the M cones 2.0e-7 (computed from
#: the bundled table, 2026-10-09), so a spectrum may stop there.
SPECTRA_COVER = (390.0, 780.0)
#: The widest step between a spectrum's wavelengths, nm: the CIE table's own.
SPECTRA_STEP = 5.0


@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class Spectra:
    """Each primary's spectral radiance at full drive, W·sr⁻¹·m⁻²·nm⁻¹, at the wavelengths `nm`
    the spectroradiometer sampled (engine spec §7.9: "The spectroradiometer's spectra are stored in
    each calibration, so any named set converts from them"). **The record holds what was
    measured**: the cone tables are interpolated onto these wavelengths, never these onto the
    tables' (`cones.excitations`)."""

    nm: tuple
    red: tuple
    green: tuple
    blue: tuple

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        nm = self.nm
        if not (isinstance(nm, tuple) and len(nm) >= 2 and all(_finite_number(v) for v in nm)):
            raise ValueError("a calibration's spectra are sampled at a tuple of finite wavelengths")
        if any(later <= earlier for earlier, later in zip(nm, nm[1:])):
            raise ValueError("a calibration's spectra's wavelengths rise strictly")
        if nm[0] > SPECTRA_COVER[0] or nm[-1] < SPECTRA_COVER[1]:
            raise ValueError(
                f"a calibration's spectra cover {SPECTRA_COVER[0]:g} to {SPECTRA_COVER[1]:g} nm; "
                f"these run from {nm[0]:g} to {nm[-1]:g}"
            )
        widest = max(later - earlier for earlier, later in zip(nm, nm[1:]))
        if widest > SPECTRA_STEP:
            raise ValueError(
                f"a calibration's spectra are sampled at least every {SPECTRA_STEP:g} nm, the CIE "
                f"table's step; these leave a gap of {widest:g} nm"
            )
        for channel in _CHANNELS:
            values = getattr(self, channel)
```

```python
def _inverse3(m) -> tuple:
    """The inverse of a 3×3 given row by row, by its adjugate."""
    det = _det3(m)
    if abs(det) < 1e-12:
        raise ValueError("a 3×3 with no inverse: its rows are not independent")
    cofactor = [[(m[(i + 1) % 3][(j + 1) % 3] * m[(i + 2) % 3][(j + 2) % 3]
                  - m[(i + 1) % 3][(j + 2) % 3] * m[(i + 2) % 3][(j + 1) % 3])
                 for j in range(3)] for i in range(3)]
    return tuple(tuple(cofactor[j][i] / det for j in range(3)) for i in range(3))


def _product3(a, b) -> tuple:
    """`a` times `b`, two 3×3 given row by row."""
    return tuple(tuple(sum(a[i][k] * b[k][j] for k in range(3)) for j in range(3))
                 for i in range(3))


def _apply3(m, v) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """A 3×3, row by row, times a vector."""
    return tuple(sum(m[i][k] * v[k] for k in range(3)) for i in range(3))


def cone_contrast(color, panel: "Calibration", background: xyY) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """ΔL/L, ΔM/M and ΔS/S of a cone color about `background` on `panel`, in the lab's observer.

    **A `DKL` color is read as the PI's hybrid** (A2's Q1, "Hybrid"; COL-07)
    [@brainard1996cone, pp. 571-572]: `lum` is a luminance contrast, the background scaled, so
    each cone changes by `lum`; `l_m` and `s_lm` are pooled cone contrast, the root of the sum of
    the three contrasts squared [@brainard1996cone, p. 568, Eq. A.4.2], along their isolating
    directions. The `l_m` direction changes L and M and not S, at the ratio that keeps V_F,10
    (`cones.V_F10`) where the background has it, so `lum=0` is isoluminant by construction in the
    lab's observer (A2's Q5); the `s_lm` direction changes S alone, which V_F,10 does not see.
    **Signs** (the engine A2 plan's call 14): `+l_m` raises L and lowers M (toward red), `+s_lm`
    raises S (toward violet), `+lum` brightens. Every component a number: `screen.resolve`
    binds a task's parameters first, and `check` reads each value one can take."""
    return _contrast(color, background_cones(panel, background))


def background_cones(panel: "Calibration", background: xyY) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """The lab observer's cone excitations of `background` on `panel`. **Refused on black**: a
    cone color is a contrast about its background, and black has none to be relative to (engine
    spec §7.5; COL-09). Refused too on a measured calibration with no spectra."""
    return _excited(_cone_matrix(panel), background)


def cone_xyz(color, panel: "Calibration", background: xyY) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """CIE XYZ, Y in cd/m², of a cone color against `background` on `panel`
    [@brainard2002display, pp. 177-178, Eq. 14]: the background's excitations, each scaled by one
    plus its contrast (`cone_contrast`), back through `Calibration.cones`."""
    matrix = _cone_matrix(panel)
    excited = _excited(matrix, background)
    contrast = _contrast(color, excited)
    return _apply3(_inverse3(matrix), tuple(e * (1.0 + c) for e, c in zip(excited, contrast)))


def _cone_matrix(panel: "Calibration") -> tuple:
    matrix = panel.cones
    if matrix is None:
        raise ValueError(
            f"calibration {panel.id or panel.measured_on} was measured without spectra, so no cone "
            f"color converts against it: a measured calibration converts cone colors from its "
            f"primaries' spectra (engine spec §7.9)"
        )
    return matrix


def _excited(matrix, background: xyY) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    if background.Y <= 0.0:
        raise ValueError(
            "a cone color is relative to its background, and this background is black (engine "
            "spec §7.5)"
        )
    return _apply3(matrix, _XYZ(background))


def _contrast(color, excited) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    if not isinstance(color, DKL):
        raise TypeError(f"{type(color).__name__} is not a cone color")
    from wl_xcon import cones

    ratio = (cones.V_F10[0] * excited[0]) / (cones.V_F10[1] * excited[1])
    norm = math.sqrt(1.0 + ratio * ratio)
    lum, l_m, s_lm = float(color.lum), float(color.l_m), float(color.s_lm)
    return (lum + l_m / norm, lum - l_m * ratio / norm, lum + s_lm)
```

```python
def unrealizable(color: Color, panel: Calibration, background: xyY | None = None) -> str | None:
    """Why `panel` cannot produce `color`, or `None` if it can: **by full conversion to primary
    weights, for every space** (engine spec §7.4; COL-08) [@brainard2002display, pp. 177-178,
    Eq. 14]. Each weight must lie between what its channel gives undriven (`floors`, 0 on an
    emissive black) and 1, within `TOLERANCE`. A cone color is converted against `background`
    first (`cone_xyz`), so it is realizable only against one: the same `DKL` fits on a mid gray
    and not on a bright one (COL-10). This replaces the `max_cone_contrast` gate, which passed
    red-green contrasts no panel could make (the science review's 7(b); the A2 research note §3)."""
    try:
        if isinstance(color, CONE_COLORS):
            if background is None:
                return "it is relative to the background, and no background was given"
            xyz = cone_xyz(color, panel, background)
        elif isinstance(color, xyY):
            xyz = _XYZ(color)
        else:
            return None
        weights = panel.weights_of(xyz)
    except ValueError as exc:
        return str(exc)
    floors = panel.floors()
    if any(w < floor - TOLERANCE or w > 1.0 + TOLERANCE for w, floor in zip(weights, floors)):
        reach = ("[0, 1]" if not any(floors) else
                 f"what each channel makes, from its undriven light "
                 f"({', '.join(f'{f:g}' for f in floors)}) to 1")
        return (
            f"needs primary weights {tuple(round(w, 3) for w in weights)}, which are outside "
            f"{reach}; the panel would clip, and a clipped color is neither the requested "
            f"chromaticity nor the requested luminance"
        )
    return None
```

```python
def _spectra(value: object) -> Spectra:
    """A record's `spectra`: `nm`, `red`, `green` and `blue`, each once, each a list of numbers,
    held to what `Spectra` holds them to."""
    names = ("nm", *_CHANNELS)
    if not isinstance(value, dict) or sorted(value) != sorted(names):
        raise ValueError("its spectra name nm, red, green and blue, each once")
    for name in names:
        if not (isinstance(value[name], list) and all(_finite_number(v) for v in value[name])):
            raise ValueError(f"its spectra's {name} is a list of numbers")
    try:
        return Spectra(**{name: tuple(float(v) for v in value[name]) for name in names})
    except ValueError as refused:
```

```python
def _claims_isoluminance(color, params: dict[str, Param]) -> bool:
    """Whether a color claims isoluminance (the PI, N§4 batch 1: "DKL `lum=0` with a chromatic
    component"): a `DKL` whose `lum` can be 0 while `l_m` or `s_lm` can be other than 0, at some
    value of its parameters. A value that cannot be read can be either, failing closed. A
    `ConeContrast` states cone contrasts, not a luminance, and claims none (the engine A2 plan's
    call 15), though one whose L and M sit at V_F,10's ratio is isoluminant by arithmetic."""
    if not isinstance(color, DKL):
        return False
    return (_can_be(color.lum, params, lambda v: v == 0.0, lambda lo, hi: lo <= 0.0 <= hi)
            and any(_can_be(v, params, lambda v: v != 0.0, lambda lo, hi: lo != 0.0 or hi != 0.0)
                    for v in (color.l_m, color.s_lm)))


def _can_be(value, params: dict[str, Param], holds, spans) -> bool:
    """Whether `value` can satisfy `holds`: a literal number itself, a parameter any of its
    choices or, with a range, `spans(low, high)`. Anything else can, failing closed."""
    if isinstance(value, P):
        param = params.get(value.name)
        if param is not None and param.choices:
            return any(not _is_number(c) or holds(float(c)) for c in param.choices)
        if param is not None and param.low is not None and param.high is not None:
            return spans(float(param.low), float(param.high))
        return True
    return not _is_number(value) or holds(float(value))
```

```python
def _modulation_faults(trial: Trial, params: dict[str, Param], panel: Calibration,
                       backgrounds: list) -> list[Finding]:
    """**The lights a contrast makes, held to the panel's reach** (engine spec §7.4, "for every
    space"; the A2 research note §3, step 5: "check both extremes of a grating"): a flat
    `Weber` light is its background times one plus its contrast, a sine grating's brightest and
    darkest bars its mean times one plus and one minus its `Michelson` contrast -- the mean it
    declares, absolute or a cone color on each background, or else each lit background. At each
    value a parameter can take. **Against the background only** (the engine A2 plan's call 18):
    drawn over another stimulus, a pattern's mean is that stimulus's light, and a sum of added
    ones is not known here; the exact drawer refuses a pixel the panel cannot make
    (`exact.output_levels`; XC-??? (lights over lights)). A window, a scotoma and a multiplier
    draw no light of their own and are left out."""
    from wl_xcon.photometry import Michelson, Weber, cone_xyz
    from wl_xcon.screen import LIGHTLESS

    combined = _combined(trial, params)
    findings: list[Finding] = []

    def held(what: str, base: xyY, factor: float, said: str) -> None:
        light = xyY(base.x, base.y, base.Y * factor)
        why = unrealizable(light, panel)
        if why is not None:
            finding = Finding("unrealizable-color", f"{what}'s {said} reaches {light}: {why}")
            if finding not in findings:
                findings.append(finding)

    for looks in _appearances(trial):
        how = [combine for shown, combine in combined if shown == looks]
        if how and all(combine == "multiply" or combine in LIGHTLESS for combine in how):
            continue
        light = _light(looks)
        if light is None:
            continue
        kind, _, contrast, mean = light
        what = type(looks).__name__
        try:
            contrasts = [c for c in _options(contrast, params) if isinstance(c, (Weber, Michelson))]
            means = [None] if mean is None else _options(mean, params)
            values = {id(c): _reach(c.value, params) for c in contrasts}
        except _Unbounded:
            continue  # refused where it is read: it cannot be bounded
        for c in contrasts:
            for value in values[id(c)]:
                if kind == "flat" and isinstance(c, Weber):
                    for background in backgrounds:
                        held(what, background, 1.0 + value,
                             f"Weber contrast of {value:g} on a background of {background}")
                if kind != "grating" or not isinstance(c, Michelson):
                    continue
                for declared in means:
                    if declared is None:
                        bases = [(b, f"mean, its background {b}") for b in backgrounds]
                    elif isinstance(declared, CONE_COLORS):
                        bases = []
                        for b in backgrounds:
                            try:
                                X, Y, Z = cone_xyz(declared, panel, b)
                            except ValueError:
                                continue  # the mean itself is refused where it is checked
                            if Y > 0.0:
                                bases.append((xyY(X / (X + Y + Z), Y / (X + Y + Z), Y),
                                              f"mean {declared} on {b}"))
                    else:
                        try:
                            bases = [(m, f"mean {m}") for m in _lights(declared, params)
                                     if isinstance(m, xyY) and m.Y > 0.0]
                        except _Unbounded:
                            continue
                    for base, said in bases:
                        for sign, bar in ((1.0, "brightest"), (-1.0, "darkest")):
                            held(what, base, 1.0 + sign * value,
                                 f"{bar} bar at Michelson {value:g} about its {said}")
    return findings
```

```python
    """An absolute light, its parameters bound, as `xyY`; `None` stays `None` (black). A cone
    color is not one: a background is what it is relative to, and `check` refuses one there."""
```

```python
def resolve(visible, values, trial, geometry, *, frame_period, frame=0, onsets=None,
            calibration=None) -> Screen:
    """The screen as `visible` and `values` make it at `frame`. `visible` keeps the order
    stimuli were shown (the trial loop's `dict`), which breaks ties within a layer;
    `onsets` gives each stimulus's first frame, from which drift is timed. **A cone color
    (`DKL`, `ConeContrast`) converts against the background through `calibration`** (engine
    build A2), so a trial with one needs it; the screen description itself stays CIE XYZ."""

    left = _absolute(trial.background_left, values) or base
    right = _absolute(trial.background_right, values) or base

    items.sort(key=lambda i: (i.layer, i.order))
    return Screen(setup=geometry.view, periphery=trial.periphery,
                  background_left=to_xyz(left) if left else _BLACK,
                  background_right=to_xyz(right) if right else _BLACK,
                  items=tuple(items), frame=frame, frame_period=frame_period)
```

```python
def output_levels(image, calibration):
    """Each pixel of a drawn eye, `(rows, cols, 3)` CIE XYZ in cd/m² (`draw`), as the output
    levels that make it on `calibration`'s panel: red, green and blue, each a fraction of the
    output's full scale, 0 to 1 (engine spec §4.9: "into the output levels that eye must see").

    The primary weights that make each pixel (as `Calibration.weights` solves them), then **each
    channel's measured transfer read backwards** (spec §7.6, `inverse_transfer`). **A pixel the
    panel cannot make raises**, naming how many and the farthest weight, rather than being
    clipped (spec §7.4); `check` refuses at load every such light it can see
    (`unrealizable-color`). **Continuous, never quantized** (the engine A2 plan's call 19):
    rounding to the output's codes, and dithering if it is the fallback (spec §7.8), are the
    display process's (engine build E), held to this within one output level (spec §10.1)."""
    primaries = np.array([to_xyz(p) for p in (calibration.red, calibration.green,
                                              calibration.blue)]).T
    weights = np.linalg.solve(primaries, np.asarray(image, dtype=float).reshape(-1, 3).T).T
    floors = np.array(calibration.floors())
    outside = (weights < floors - TOLERANCE) | (weights > 1.0 + TOLERANCE)
    if outside.any():
        pixels = int(outside.any(axis=1).sum())
        worst = weights[outside][np.argmax(np.abs(weights[outside] - 0.5))]
        raise ValueError(
            f"{pixels} pixel(s) need a primary weight the panel cannot make (the farthest "
            f"{worst:g}, against each channel's undriven light and 1); `check` refuses a light it "
            f"can see that does this, and this one it could not"
        )
    out = np.empty_like(weights)
    for channel, transfer in enumerate(calibration.transfer):
        out[:, channel] = inverse_transfer(transfer, np.clip(weights[:, channel],
                                                             transfer.fractions[0], 1.0))
    return out.reshape(np.shape(image))


def inverse_transfer(transfer, weights):
    """The level at which `transfer` gives each of `weights` (a fraction of full drive's light):
    between its measured points by straight lines, as it is read forwards, and **where the table
    is flat, the lowest level that gives that light**. Every weight lies between the table's
    first fraction and 1; `output_levels` refuses any other first."""
    fractions = np.asarray(transfer.fractions, dtype=float)
    steps = np.asarray(transfer.levels, dtype=float)
    w = np.asarray(weights, dtype=float)
    # The first point whose light is not less than w, so a flat run is entered at its start, and
    # the last point whose light is less: w lies between them, or is the first exactly.
    at = np.minimum(np.searchsorted(fractions, w, side="left"), len(fractions) - 1)
    below = np.maximum(at - 1, 0)
    span = fractions[at] - fractions[below]
    share = np.where(span > 0, (w - fractions[below]) / np.where(span > 0, span, 1.0), 0.0)
    return steps[below] + share * (steps[at] - steps[below])
```
